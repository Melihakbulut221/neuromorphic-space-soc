# 04 — Technology selection, memory strategy, flow and cost

Research-phase report. Scope: choose the 130 nm implementation technology,
define the on-chip memory strategy, assess the open-source flow at SoC
scale, and bound the cost of getting to first silicon.

Convention used throughout: statements marked **[fact]** are backed by a
cited public source; statements marked **[estimate]** are engineering
judgment or extrapolation and must be re-verified before they gate a
decision. Where the 2026 status of a service or tool is uncertain, this is
said explicitly.

Summary of the recommendation (argued in section 3):

- **Primary target: IHP SG13G2** (130 nm SiGe BiCMOS, open PDK) — the only
  open-PDK 130 nm option with published radiation characterization on its
  process family and a European institutional shuttle path, which is the
  strategic asset for a space-positioned part.
- **Fallback: SkyWater SKY130** (via Tiny Tapeout and ChipFoundry
  chipIgnite) — the developer's flow is already silicon-proven here, and
  the commercial MPW machinery is more mature; it is the schedule insurance
  if SG13G2 open-flow blockers persist at the tapeout gate.
- Block-level pipe-cleaning continues on **both** Tiny Tapeout variants
  (SKY130 and IHP), which is cheap and keeps the fallback warm.

## 1. 130 nm technology options

### 1.1 SkyWater SKY130

**Process.** 180 nm–130 nm hybrid bulk CMOS ("130 nm" node class), 1.8 V
core, 5 metal layers, open PDK originally released by Google and SkyWater
(https://github.com/google/skywater-pdk). **[fact]**

**Open-flow maturity.** The most mature open-PDK digital flow that exists:
OpenLane/LibreLane and OpenROAD-flow-scripts both treat sky130 as the
reference platform, and hundreds of designs have been fabricated through
Efabless MPWs, chipIgnite, and Tiny Tapeout. **[fact]** The developer's
own rootless OpenROAD/LibreLane toolchain is proven on SKY130 end to end
(Tiny Tapeout submissions plus a full MVP GDS that is DRC/LVS/antenna
clean), which makes this the zero-flow-risk option for this team.
**[fact, project-internal]**

**2026 shuttle landscape.** This changed substantially after the Efabless
shutdown (announced insolvency/closure in 2025):

- **ChipFoundry** (chipfoundry.io), founded by the Efabless founders,
  re-established access to the SkyWater 130 nm process with the open PDK.
  Their chipIgnite program offers standard shuttle participation at
  **$14,950 per project**, including **100 QFN-packaged parts** (or all
  bare die), 10 of them assembled on M.2 daughter cards, plus one Caravel
  evaluation board; an additional 50 bare die costs **$3,000**. Harness
  options: Caravel/Caravan with ~10 mm² user area, OpenFrame with ~15 mm²
  user area, Caravel Mini with ~2 mm². **Three shuttles are planned for
  2026**, dates demand-driven (https://chipfoundry.io/faqs). **[fact]**
  Note this is a price increase over the historical Efabless chipIgnite
  ($9,750 at launch, later ~$10k;
  https://www.embedded.com/efabless-chipignite-enables-soc-for-9750-in-skywater-cmos-node/).
  **[fact]**
- **Tiny Tapeout** survived the Efabless closure and now runs on
  ChipFoundry SKY130 shuttles (ChipCreate MPW; e.g. the CC2509 shuttle
  got an early GO for September 2026,
  https://tinytapeout.com/news/sky130-confirmed/). Multiple SKY130
  shuttles are active or queued in 2026 (ttsky26a, ttsky26b, ttsky26c;
  https://app.tinytapeout.com/shuttles/ttsky26a). Reported pricing is
  around **€70 per tile** and **€300 for a devkit** (reported by
  eeNews Europe,
  https://www.eenewseurope.com/en/tiny-tapeout-sees-industrial-boost-as-it-recovers-from-efabless-closure/),
  with exact prices per shuttle in the calculator
  (https://app.tinytapeout.com/calculator). For the first time designs may
  be up to 4 tiles high, and two shuttles run simultaneously. **[fact]**
  A Tiny Tapeout tile is approximately 160 µm x 100 µm (~0.016 mm²), so
  even a large multi-tile design is a block-level test vehicle, not an
  SoC. **[fact for tile size order; estimate for exact 2026 dimensions —
  check https://tinytapeout.com/specs/ per shuttle]**

**Risks.** (a) No public radiation characterization of SKY130 is known to
this project — searches find no published TID/SEE data for the open PDK
process; SkyWater markets radiation hardening as a separate
rad-hard-by-design activity on other nodes. Any radiation claim on SKY130
would have to be generated by this project's own test campaign.
**[fact that no data was found; absence of data is not data of absence]**
(b) The original google/skywater-pdk repository appears to have been
archived in 2026, with maintenance continuing in downstream repositories
(open_pdks, ChipFoundry forks) — a governance signal worth watching, not
an immediate technical blocker.
**[uncertain — verify at https://github.com/google/skywater-pdk]**
(c) The post-Efabless ecosystem is one company deep again: chipIgnite
continuity depends on ChipFoundry's commercial success.

### 1.2 IHP SG13G2

**Process.** IHP (Leibniz Institute, Frankfurt/Oder, Germany) 130 nm
SiGe:C BiCMOS, 1.2 V core CMOS + 3.3 V I/O, 7 aluminum metal layers
including two thick metals, plus very fast HBTs (fT/fmax up to
~300/500 GHz — irrelevant to this digital design but the reason the PDK
exists). Open PDK at https://github.com/IHP-GmbH/IHP-Open-PDK, docs at
https://ihp-open-pdk-docs.readthedocs.io/. **[fact]**

**Open-flow maturity.** Younger than sky130 but moving fast:
OpenROAD-flow-scripts carries an `ihp-sg13g2` platform, LibreLane
integration exists and is used for digital hardening and top-level
assembly (see the IIC-JKU mixed-signal template,
https://github.com/iic-jku/ihp-sg13g2-ams-chip-template, and
https://wiki.f-si.org/index.php?title=IHP_Open_Pdk_integration_with_Magic,_Netgen,_and_LibreLane),
and Tiny Tapeout has run several IHP shuttles (ttihp25a, ttihp25b,
ttihp26a — the latter closed March 2026 with 283 designs,
https://tinytapeout.com/chips/ttihp26a/). **[fact]** However, IHP's own
documentation still describes the open PDK as an early-access version not
yet scheduled for production purposes
(https://ihp-open-pdk-docs.readthedocs.io/), and there are open integration
issues around the foundry SRAM macros (LVS failures:
https://github.com/IHP-GmbH/IHP-Open-PDK/issues/239; GDS merge of bitkit
cells in ORFS:
https://github.com/The-OpenROAD-Project/OpenROAD-flow-scripts/discussions/3014).
**[fact]** These are flow-integration issues, not silicon issues — the
same macros are production items in the commercial PDK — but they are
exactly the kind of issue that costs weeks at tapeout time. **[estimate]**

**Radiation data — the strategic point.** IHP has published radiation
characterization of its SiGe BiCMOS platform for space, including an ESA
AMICSA paper covering the SGB25RH (250 nm) and SG13RH (130 nm) rad-hard
PDKs (Cirillo et al., "Radiation-Hardened SiGe BiCMOS Technologies for
Analogue and Mixed-Signal ICs",
https://indico.esa.int/event/102/contributions/43/attachments/105/126/AMICSA_IHP_SiGe_BiCMOS_for_Space_Applications_final.pdf).
Concrete published results **[fact, from that paper]**:

- SG13RH HBTs show no substantial TID variation up to a tested dose of
  **1210 krad(Si)**.
- SG13RH LV-NMOS devices used in the standard-cell libraries pass TID
  levels up to **110 krad(Si)** with leakage within specification; PMOS
  devices tested to 500 krad(Si) with no variation; enclosed-layout (ELT)
  NMOS stable well beyond 500 krad(Si) (shown to 900 krad).
- **No SEL detected up to 65 MeV·cm²/mg** on the CMOS standard-cell
  library test vehicles (heavy ions).
- SEU cross-sections measured on 1024-bit shift registers with reference,
  DICE, and TMR flip-flops; the rad-hard library extensions (~80 cells for
  SG13S/SG13RH) include TMR-FF and DICE-FF cells.
- ESCC-2269010 evaluation was completed for SGB25RH and was in progress
  for SG13RH, with EPPL listing targeted around 2018–2020.

Caveat, stated honestly: **SG13RH is built on SG13S**, the sibling of
SG13G2 in the same 130 nm SG13 family (shared CMOS backbone, different
HBT module). The published numbers characterize the family's 130 nm CMOS,
not the open SG13G2 PDK specifically, and the open PDK does not ship the
rad-hard (DICE/TMR/ELT) library extensions. The correct claim for this
project is therefore: "implemented on a 130 nm process family with
published ESCC-track TID/SEL characterization; device target LEO
10–30 krad(Si) class, fault-tolerant by architecture" — not "on a
rad-hard PDK." The claim wording itself carries no numeric TID figure
(docs/05 section 4 rule 3: positioning stays below 100 krad(Si) in
wording as well as in fact); the platform numbers live only in the
sourced-facts list above, with attribution. **[fact + framing]**
That is still categorically more than SKY130 offers, and 110 krad(Si)
standard-cell-level TID comfortably covers LEO CubeSat missions (typical
mission doses of a few to tens of krad behind 2–3 mm Al). **[estimate for
mission dose; standard figure]**

Separately, third-party literature adds radiation data on the same
130 nm SiGe BiCMOS technology base — not on SG13G2 circuits. A 112 Gb/s
rad-hard optical transceiver was designed in SG13RH, the rad-hard
sibling PDK; the paper does not irradiate the transceiver itself but
cites prior technology characterization of SiGe HBTs to 1.2 Mrad(Si)
TID (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/351632595). JICG
transistor structures in 130 nm SiGe BiCMOS were verified against
TID-induced leakage above 1.3 Mrad(Si), with CMOS inverter SET LET
threshold above 130 MeV·cm²/mg
(https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0168900220312298).
**[fact, from those papers; results are for SG13RH and transistor/test
structures, not SG13G2 circuits]**

**2026 shuttle access and cost.** Via Europractice
(https://europractice-ic.com/schedules-prices-2026/):

- Standard SG13G2 MPW: **€7,300/mm²** (discounted **€6,205/mm²** for
  eligible academics); IHP's own price list confirms €7,300/mm² with a
  **10 mm² minimum** and **40 diced samples** delivered
  (https://www.ihp-microelectronics.com/services/research-and-prototyping-service/mpw-prototyping-service/schedule-price-list).
  **[fact]**
- **Open-source MPW pricing**: for designs meeting open-source
  requirements with a signed Open Silicon MPW Program Participation
  Agreement, **€2,800/mm²** (run cumulative area >100 mm²) dropping to
  **€1,000/mm²** (>150 mm²) (https://europractice-ic.com/schedules-prices-2026/).
  **[fact]** The realized per-project price therefore depends on how full
  the open-source run is — treat €1,000–2,800/mm² as the planning band.
  **[estimate]**
- Roughly three SG13G2 runs per year; fab cycle time from tape-in to
  shipment is on the order of 6 months (e.g. tape-in Aug 2026 → shipment
  Feb 2027 per IHP's schedule). **[fact]**
- Tiny Tapeout's IHP shuttles provide the same €-per-tile entry point for
  block-level tests on this exact process. **[fact]**

## 2. Technology recommendation

**Primary: IHP SG13G2. Fallback: SkyWater SKY130.**

Reasoning:

1. **The radiation story is the product.** This is a fault-tolerant SoC
   for space. On SG13G2 the datasheet-level claim is "130 nm family with
   published ESCC-track TID and SEL characterization" plus this project's
   architectural hardening.

   **Corrected 2026-08-29: the number does not belong in the claim
   sentence.** An earlier revision recommended saying "no SEL to
   65 MeV·cm²/mg" as part of it. The figure is real, but it measures
   SG13RH/SG13S standard-cell test vehicles — not this device, and not
   the open SG13G2 PDK — and a reader of that sentence would reasonably
   take it as a property of the part being described. `docs/07` finding
   F-8 corrected exactly this defect once already, in a different
   sentence, where TID figures at or above 100 krad had been embedded in
   recommended wording against the binding rule in `docs/05` section 4.
   Cite the family characterization, attribute it, and keep the numbers
   in the sourced-facts section where their attribution travels with
   them. Nothing in this repository measures the single-event latch-up
   behaviour of anything this project has built.

   On SKY130 the claim reduces to
   "architectural hardening on an uncharacterized bulk process." For
   customers, reviewers, and any future funding conversation, the first
   claim is materially stronger. (Doc 00, observation 5: bulk 130 nm has
   no intrinsic SEL immunity — the SG13 family's published no-SEL-to-65
   heavy-ion result (SG13RH/SG13S standard-cell test vehicles, section
   1.2) retires the scariest bulk-CMOS risk, transferring to SG13G2 only
   via the shared 130 nm CMOS backbone.)
2. **European space ecosystem.** IHP is an ESA-adjacent European
   institute with EPPL-track processes and Europractice access;
   a European CubeSat-supplier customer base (doc 05) aligns with a
   European foundry and shuttle path.
3. **Foundry SRAM macros exist in the open PDK** (section 3), which is a
   real density and yield advantage over sky130's compiler-generated
   SRAM.
4. **Cost is comparable or better** at MVP die sizes under the
   open-source MPW pricing (section 5).
5. **The fallback is unusually strong.** Because the developer's flow is
   already proven on SKY130 with a clean full-chip GDS, falling back
   costs a PDK retarget of the RTL-to-GDS scripts, not a flow rebuild.
   The RTL is process-independent; floorplan and memory strategy are the
   main rework items. **[estimate: 3–6 weeks of retarget effort]**

Gate condition for the fallback: if, at the MVP tapeout-decision gate,
the SG13G2 open flow still has unresolved blockers on (a) SRAM macro
LVS/GDS integration, (b) DRC-clean full-chip assembly with LibreLane/ORFS,
or (c) shuttle slot availability inside the schedule window, the MVP die
moves to SKY130/chipIgnite and SG13G2 becomes the flight-path target for
the following iteration. Block-level Tiny Tapeout submissions should be
run on **both** ttsky and ttihp shuttles until that gate, so the fallback
stays exercised. **[plan]**

## 3. Memory strategy at 130 nm

The GR801 carries 11.2 MB of on-chip SRAM at 28 nm; doc 00 already
concludes the retarget lands in the hundreds-of-kB class. This section
makes that concrete.

### 3.1 SRAM macro options, 2026 status

**SKY130 / OpenRAM.** OpenRAM (https://github.com/VLSIDA/OpenRAM) can
generate sky130 SRAM macros, and pre-hardened macros exist
(https://github.com/efabless/sky130_sram_macros, e.g.
`sky130_sram_2kbyte_1rw1r_32x512_8`). Status honestly stated:

- The sky130 SRAM cell needs a special DRC ruleset (optical proximity
  tricks); the standard DRC decks flag false errors, and LVS mismatch
  issues were still being reported against OpenRAM sky130 builds as of
  2026 (https://github.com/VLSIDA/OpenRAM/issues/292). **[fact]**
- The ecosystem is drifting away from OpenRAM for sky130: the Berkeley
  Hammer flow states that from v1.0.2 only SRAM22-generated macros are
  supported (https://hammer-vlsi.readthedocs.io/en/stable/Technology/Sky130.html;
  SRAM22: https://github.com/rahulk29/sram22). **[fact]**
- Yield/timing reputation: the 2 kB macro has been fabbed repeatedly on
  Efabless MPWs with mixed anecdotal results (timing model optimism,
  per-instance failures reported by users); no systematic public yield
  data exists. **[estimate/reputation — treat every sky130 SRAM instance
  as needing ECC plus a BIST-based map-out capability]**
- Density: the 2 kB OpenRAM macro is roughly 0.28–0.35 mm² including
  periphery, i.e. **~6–7 KiB/mm²**. **[estimate from macro dimensions]**

**IHP SG13G2 foundry SRAM.** The open PDK ships IHP's compiled SRAM
macros (`RM_IHPSG13_1P_*`, e.g. `RM_IHPSG13_1P_1024x32_c2_bm_bist` —
single-port, byte-masked, **with built-in BIST**), which are production
items in the commercial PDK
(https://ihp-open-pdk-docs.readthedocs.io/). **[fact]** This is the
strongest memory argument for SG13G2: real foundry bitcells with a BIST
interface, versus compiler-generated cells on sky130. Open issues are in
flow integration (LVS abstracts, GDS merge of bitkit cells — see section
1.2 citations), not in the silicon. **[fact]** Density of these macros is
not publicly tabulated; commercial 130 nm compiled SRAM typically lands
at **~25–40 KiB/mm²** including periphery, and that band should be
assumed until measured from the LEF. **[estimate]**

**DFFRAM.** The AUCOHL DFFRAM compiler
(https://github.com/AUCOHL/DFFRAM) builds RAM from standard-cell
latches/DFFs with dense manual placement; sky130A is silicon-proven and
the project remained active through 2025. **[fact]** Cost: flip-flop
RAM runs on the order of 25–40 µm²/bit at 130 nm even with dense packing,
i.e. **~3–5 KiB/mm²** — 5–10x worse than real SRAM. **[estimate]** Its
role here is small, radiation-friendly memories: register files, FIFOs,
and any array below ~4 KiB, where a flop-based array with TMR or ECC is
simpler to harden and to fault-inject than an SRAM macro. **[plan]**

### 3.2 Realistic on-chip SRAM budget

For a 10–50 mm² class die, with interface logic, CPU, and the SNN fabric
taking meaningful area:

| Die class | Platform example | SRAM budget (with ECC overhead) |
|---|---|---|
| ~10–15 mm² user area | chipIgnite Caravel/OpenFrame | 32–96 KiB on sky130 (OpenRAM density); 128–256 KiB on SG13G2 (foundry macros) **[estimate]** |
| ~25 mm² full-custom die | IHP MPW, 25 mm² | ~200–330 KiB on SG13G2 with roughly a third of the die in SRAM (~8.3 mm² at the section 3.1 band of 25–40 KiB/mm²); 512 KiB would take ~13–20 mm², i.e. half the die or more **[estimate]** |
| ~50 mm² | IHP MPW, upper cost bound | up to ~1 MiB, but cost-prohibitive for an MVP (section 5) **[estimate]** |

ECC (SEC-DED, 32+7 or 64+8) costs ~22% (32-bit) or ~13% (64-bit) extra
bits plus corner logic; scrubbing is a controller, not an area item.
**[fact for code overheads]** The working target from doc 00 —
"hundreds of kB class" — is achievable on SG13G2 at ~25 mm² and only
marginally on sky130 at chipIgnite sizes. This asymmetry is itself an
argument for the primary/fallback ordering: the fallback MVP on sky130
would carry a smaller memory (e.g. 64 KiB CPU + 64 KiB SNN weight/state)
and lean harder on external memory.

### 3.3 QSPI XIP as the large-model path

Mirroring GR801's QSPI controller (2 chip selects, doc 00): large-model
weights live off-chip in QSPI NOR flash (or QSPI PSRAM for mutable
state), with the SNN fabric streaming weights per multi-pass layer
group rather than holding the whole model on-die.

- Bandwidth: QSPI at 100 MHz SDR, 4 lanes = **50 MB/s**; DDR doubles
  that. At 4-bit weights, 50 MB/s streams 100 M weights/s — for an
  event-driven fabric running at tens of MHz with sparse activation,
  weight streaming, not compute, becomes the layer-switch latency
  bottleneck, which multi-pass scheduling must hide. **[estimate;
  arithmetic is exact, the bottleneck claim needs the doc 02 fabric
  sizing to confirm]**
- Radiation posture: NOR flash cells are TID-soft above a few tens of
  krad and need mission-level screening, but weight images are
  read-mostly and CRC/ECC-framed re-reads plus a golden image with
  scrub-on-boot give a serviceable CubeSat-class story. Rad-tolerant
  NOR (e.g. automotive/space-screened parts) is a BOM decision, not a
  die decision. **[estimate/standard practice]**
- XIP for the RV32 manager: code executes in place from the same QSPI
  with an ECC-protected I-cache/tightly-coupled RAM on-die, which is
  exactly the GR801 pattern scaled down. **[plan]**

## 4. Flow and verification

### 4.1 OpenROAD / LibreLane at SoC scale

- **Flat vs hierarchical.** OpenROAD comfortably closes flat designs in
  the low-hundreds-of-thousands of instances at 130 nm; this SoC (RV32
  core + SNN fabric + interfaces) plausibly lands at 150k–400k instances
  depending on fabric sizing. **[estimate]** The proven open-flow pattern
  at this scale is **hierarchical hardening**: harden the SNN tile / node
  as a macro once, instantiate it N times, and assemble at top level —
  this is the Caravel/Tiny Tapeout pattern and the one LibreLane supports
  well (macro hardening, padframe generation, top-level assembly; see the
  IIC-JKU sg13g2 template,
  https://iic-jku.github.io/ihp-sg13g2-ams-chip-template/). **[fact for
  the pattern]** The tile-replication structure of the SNN fabric (doc
  02) makes it a natural fit, and it matches the developer's prior
  tile-based Tiny Tapeout experience.
- **Macro placement.** SRAM macros and hardened tiles are placed manually
  (ORFS `macro_place` halo/channel parameters or explicit placement);
  automated macro placement in OpenROAD is usable but reviewed by hand at
  this die complexity. Power-grid stitching across macro rows and
  antenna/density fill at the top level are the standard pain points.
  **[fact for flow features; estimate for pain ranking]**

### 4.2 Clock and power at 25–100 MHz

- 25–50 MHz closes trivially on sky130 HD and on sg13g2 standard cells
  in slow corners; 100 MHz is attainable for the RV32 + interconnect with
  pipelining discipline but will require timing-driven iteration on the
  SNN fabric's event arbitration paths. **[estimate, consistent with
  published open-flow results on both PDKs]**
- Power at 1.8 V sky130 / 1.2 V sg13g2, order-of-magnitude planning
  numbers: 20–60 µW/MHz per 10k gates of active logic; a 300k-instance
  SoC at 50 MHz with clock gating lands in the **30–150 mW** band, plus
  SRAM and I/O. sg13g2's 1.2 V core gives it a structural dynamic-power
  advantage (~2.2x lower CV² than 1.8 V at equal capacitance).
  **[estimate — to be replaced by post-synthesis power numbers]**
- The event-driven fabric's whole point is that average power is
  activity-, not clock-, dominated; clock gating on idle tiles is the
  single highest-leverage power feature and is fully supported in the
  open flow (latch-based ICG cells exist in both libraries). **[fact for
  cell availability]**

### 4.3 DFT in the open flow

The developer has prior hands-on MBIST, boundary-scan, and scan-proof
experience from a separate 130 nm-class project, so the plan assumes
hand-rolled DFT is realistic rather than aspirational.

- **Scan.** Yosys provides scan-replacement capability, and the AUCOHL
  **Fault** DFT toolchain (https://github.com/AUCOHL/Fault) adds scan
  chain insertion plus ATPG and coverage on open PDKs. Maturity is below
  commercial DFT tools; plan on flat scan chains per clock domain and
  conservative coverage targets (~90% stuck-at) rather than
  compression. **[fact for tool existence; estimate for coverage]**
- **MBIST.** Hand-rolled March-C+ MBIST wrapped around every SRAM macro
  — on SG13G2 the `_bist` foundry macros already expose a BIST
  interface, which reduces this to a controller plus reporting.
  **[fact for macro feature]** MBIST doubles as the in-orbit scrub/test
  engine, so it is a product feature, not just a manufacturing feature.
- **Boundary scan.** Hand-rolled IEEE 1149.1 TAP, reusing the prior
  project's implementation experience; needed anyway as the debug/bring-up
  spine on a die with limited pins.

### 4.4 Multi-corner STA

Practice, not theory: sign off with OpenSTA over the full PVT set shipped
in each PDK — for sky130 HD: tt_025C_1v80, ss_100C_1v60, ss_n40C_*,
ff_n40C_1v95 (the n40C corners matter: space thermal environments are
cold-biased as often as hot); for sg13g2: the typ/slow/fast liberty set at
1.08/1.2/1.32 V. **[fact that these corner sets exist; exact corner names
to be pinned per PDK version]** Additional practice for this project:
hold-check with extra margin on scan paths and on the CDC-adjacent
event-network interfaces, and a derated "aged/irradiated" run using a
flat pessimism factor (5–10%) as a crude TID-shift proxy, since neither
open PDK ships radiation-derated libraries. **[estimate/methodology]**
